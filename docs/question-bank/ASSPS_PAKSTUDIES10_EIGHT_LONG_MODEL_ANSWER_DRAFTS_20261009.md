### X-PAKS-C01-L01 — Chapter 1 · ideological basis

The movement for Pakistan drew upon several ideas that developed within the political and social circumstances of British India. Many Muslim thinkers and political leaders emphasized the importance of maintaining a distinct religious and cultural identity while gaining a meaningful voice in representative institutions. Concerns about constitutional safeguards, education and political representation became important as plans for self-government were discussed. Supporters of the Two-Nation Theory argued that Muslims and Hindus constituted distinct political communities whose interests needed separate constitutional recognition. Islamic values of social responsibility and justice also appeared in accounts of the movement. These arguments contributed to demands for political autonomy and, eventually, a separate homeland, although people in the subcontinent held diverse views about the best constitutional future.

**Separately proposed five-mark marking criteria:** Explain historical Muslim cultural and political identity · Discuss representation and constitutional safeguards · Describe Two-Nation Theory as a political argument · Identify social or ethical values invoked in the movement · Connect ideas to demands for autonomy and Pakistan without erasing differing views

### X-PAKS-C02-L01 — Chapter 2 · Pakistan Movement

During the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, educational and political activity encouraged Muslim communities in British India to debate their future role in representative government. The All-India Muslim League was founded in 1906 and became an important organization for Muslim political representation. Muhammad Iqbal and other thinkers contributed ideas about political identity and possible arrangements for Muslim-majority regions. The Lahore Resolution of March 1940 marked a major step in the League’s political demands. Negotiations and disagreements over constitutional arrangements intensified during the 1940s, alongside British plans to transfer power. The partition of British India in August 1947 resulted in the establishment of Pakistan and India and was accompanied by displacement and severe communal suffering. A careful history includes both political milestones and the human consequences of partition.

**Separately proposed five-mark marking criteria:** Explain educational and early political organization · Identify founding and role of Muslim League in 1906 · Mention major thinkers or constitutional arguments · Explain importance of 1940 Lahore Resolution · Connect negotiations and 1947 independence while recognizing partition consequences

### X-PAKS-C03-L01 — Chapter 3 · constitutional development

Pakistan’s first constitution came into force in 1956 and established a parliamentary form of government with a single central legislative chamber. It was abrogated in 1958, interrupting constitutional government. The 1962 constitution introduced a presidential system, also with a unicameral central legislature, and vested extensive executive authority in the president. That constitutional order ended in 1969. Following major political changes and the separation of East Pakistan in 1971, a new constitution was adopted in 1973. It restored a federal parliamentary form of government and created a bicameral federal Parliament consisting of the National Assembly and Senate, with constitutional roles for the prime minister and president. The 1973 Constitution, subsequently amended, remains the central legal framework, illustrating both changes in governmental design and periods of constitutional disruption.

**Separately proposed five-mark marking criteria:** 1956 parliamentary system and single chamber · 1958 abrogation and interrupted continuity · 1962 presidential arrangement and central authority · 1973 federal parliamentary and bicameral features · Current constitutional significance and impact of interruption/amendments

### X-PAKS-C04-L01 — Chapter 4 · geography

Pakistan’s physical geography influences where people live and how they work. The Indus river system and associated irrigated plains support agriculture and settlement across substantial parts of Punjab and Sindh. Mountains and uplands offer tourism, hydropower and other opportunities, but their steep terrain can make transport, services and construction more difficult. Climate differences affect rainfall, crop choices, water availability and exposure to heat, floods or droughts. The need for water storage, irrigation and reliable transport influences the location of farming, industry and towns. Urban centres also develop around markets, ports, road networks and employment opportunities. Because geography creates both advantages and constraints, sustainable management of water, land and disaster risk is important for long-term development.

**Separately proposed five-mark marking criteria:** Relief and mountain constraints and opportunities · Indus basin irrigation and fertile plains · Climate effects on agriculture and water · Connections to settlement, towns and transport · Responsible land/water and disaster-risk management

### X-PAKS-C05-L01 — Chapter 5 · international affairs

Pakistan’s international relations are shaped by its location in South Asia, links with neighbouring regions and the interests of its people. Border geography, regional stability and security concerns influence diplomatic priorities and cooperation. Economic needs also matter: access to trade routes, energy supplies, investment and markets can encourage bilateral and multilateral agreements. Relations with neighbours may involve cooperation as well as disagreements, which are best addressed through diplomacy and internationally recognized processes. Pakistan also participates in international organizations, including the United Nations, and contributes to discussions about development, humanitarian assistance and regional issues. Foreign policy therefore involves balancing national security, economic opportunity, international obligations and peaceful engagement rather than responding to geography alone.

**Separately proposed five-mark marking criteria:** Geographical location and regional links · Security and stability as diplomatic concerns · Trade, energy and investment interests · Bilateral diplomacy and peaceful dispute management · International organizations and cooperative obligations

### X-PAKS-C06-L01 — Chapter 6 · economic development

Agriculture provides food, raw materials and employment and can support related businesses such as food processing. Industry transforms materials into manufactured products, creating employment and opportunities for exports. The services sector includes transport, banking, communication, education and healthcare, helping people and businesses carry out economic activities. These sectors depend on each other: farms need transport and machinery, factories need skilled workers and reliable supplies, and service providers depend on the wider economy. Human capital means people’s knowledge, skills and health, which can improve productivity and innovation. Better infrastructure, careful use of water and energy, and opportunities for different communities support more inclusive and sustainable economic development.

**Separately proposed five-mark marking criteria:** Role of agriculture and food or raw material supply · Manufacturing and industrial value creation · Services and economic coordination · Interdependence of the major sectors · Human skills, infrastructure and sustainable growth

### X-PAKS-C07-L01 — Chapter 7 · population and society

Population size and age structure influence the need for schools, healthcare, housing and employment. A growing working-age population can contribute to development when education, skills and decent work are available, but shortages in these areas create pressure on families and services. Urbanization can bring people closer to jobs, transport and markets while also increasing demand for clean water, sanitation and affordable housing. Pakistan’s different languages, cultures and regional traditions enrich social life and provide opportunities for cultural exchange. Development planning should therefore use reliable population information, improve essential services in urban and rural areas and ensure that different communities can participate fairly. Inclusive policies and respectful communication help strengthen social cohesion.

**Separately proposed five-mark marking criteria:** Population growth and age structure effects · Education, skills and employment needs · Urban opportunities and service pressures · Value of linguistic and cultural diversity · Inclusive planning and social cohesion

### X-PAKS-C08-L01 — Chapter 8 · women empowerment

Women’s empowerment means expanding women’s real opportunities to make decisions and participate equally in education, work and public life. Access to quality schooling and further training helps develop knowledge and economic independence. Equal protection under the law, access to justice and freedom from harassment or violence are important for meaningful participation. Affordable healthcare and safe public spaces also affect the ability to learn and work. Fair access to employment, entrepreneurship, property rights and financial services can widen economic opportunities. Women should be able to contribute to community organizations and representative institutions, and their perspectives should be heard in decisions that affect them. Progress depends on removing practical barriers and supporting dignity and equal opportunity rather than relying on symbolic representation alone.

**Separately proposed five-mark marking criteria:** Education and skills opportunities · Legal equality, rights and access to justice · Health, safety and protection from harassment · Employment, entrepreneurship and economic access · Meaningful civic voice and removal of participation barriers
