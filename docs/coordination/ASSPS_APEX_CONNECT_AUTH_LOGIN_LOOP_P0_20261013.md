## 1. Direct, sanitized live observations (NO private credentials, cookies or IDs collected)
- https://api.assps.edu.pk is the **APEX Connect** Next.js login/portal (VPS Nginx forwards to 127.0.0.1:3000). https://app.assps.edu.pk is a separate ASSPS SaaS login and school management portal. Do not silently equate their sessions.
- Public GET / ->302 /login, GET /login 200, GET /health 200. Connect PM2 service and shared backend5000 are running.
- Sanitized Nginx aggregate around October 13 ~13:53 UTC showed POST /api/auth/login returning **HTTP200** in three observed requests, immediately followed by GET /api/auth/me **HTTP401** and POST /api/auth/refresh **HTTP401**; repeated login page GET 200. This is independent evidence of a session verification/redirect loop, not proof of a wrong password.
- A bounded read-only localhost request to the **backend** GET http://127.0.0.1:5000/api/auth/me observed **HTTP429**, showing strict auth attempt limiter can block even read-only session introspection. No password attempted.
- Deployed backend source src/server.js contains authLimiter 10 requests/15 minutes globally mounted on ['/api/auth','/api/admin/auth'] (includes GET /me). APEX Connect's src/proxy.ts verifyConnectSession() repeatedly forwards incoming cookies to BACKEND_URL/auth/me on login and portal navigation. When backend returns non-2xx, proxy treats session invalid and can clear authentication cookies/redirect to /login. Sessions share backend loopback/proxy traffic, so high-frequency passive checks can exhaust quota and eject legitimate users.
- Deployed APEX Connect src/context/AuthContext.tsx refreshes own /api/auth/me; DashboardLayout redirects to /login when user is absent. Session persistence/auth cookies, middleware claims and role routing are separately plausible issues, particularly for platform-admin accounts. Their exact cause for an individual account is **not** established without privacy-safe cookie presence/response instrumentation or genuine staff session.
- NEXT and backend .env config **presence/equality** checked without printing secret material: JWT_SECRET and DB_HOST/PORT/NAME/USER settings match in the respective on-disk source env files; no browser cookie or user identity accessed. Environment parity by itself does not certify browser cookies or runtime JWT.
